Day Fire

NOAA/CIRA Day Fire RGB on Himawari bands (R B07 3.9 µm thermal, scaled 27–60 °C, G B04 0.86 µm, B B03 0.64 µm), gamma 0.4/1/1. Fires show red.

  • New burn scar
  • Smoke
  • Low / warm cloud
  • Cold cloud (10.4 µm below ~290 K, added)
  • Healthy vegetation
  • Bare ground / old burn scar / urban
  • Water

Recipe from the VIIRS Day Fire RGB quick guide, with the red minimum raised from 0 to 27 °C so tropical sea stays black. Over forest, smoke and cloud come out the same teal, so cold cloud (10.4 µm below ~290 K) is shown grey; smoke and low, warm cloud keep their colour. The 0.86/0.64 µm channels are divided by cos(sun zenith), capped at 80°, so brightness does not follow the sun (it overshoots when the sun is more than ~60° from vertical; an amber low-sun note on the image marks that). Himawari's 3.9 µm band has 2 km pixels (VIIRS: 375 m), so only larger fires show. Warm, sunlit ground and sun glint on water can also look red, and thick cloud hides fires.

Smoke RGB

ASMC-style smoke and haze composite (R B03 0.64 µm, G B04 0.86 µm, B B13 10.4 µm inverted). Telling low cloud and haze from thicker or higher cloud.

14 Nov 2026 18:20 SGT
14 Nov 2026 10:20 UTC
Download PNG · Open full size
  • Haze, smoke and low (warm) cloud
  • Thick or high (cold) cloud: white / blue-white
  • Land and vegetation
  • Sea (clear)
  • Fire pixel (3.9 µm, red mark)

Land is green (strong near-infrared). Smoke, haze and low, warm cloud are yellow: they show in the visible and near-infrared channels but not in the infrared. Thick or high cloud is cold, so it is also bright in the infrared channel and comes out white or blue-white. The same colour assignment is not unique: warm, thick low cloud and fog also look yellow, so use the texture (smoke is smooth and streaky, cloud is lumpy). The ranges (red 0–45 %, green 0–65 %, blue 305–220 K) were tuned here, as the guide gives none; the 0.64/0.86 µm channels are divided by cos(sun zenith), capped at 80°, like Day Fire (an amber note marks a low sun). Red marks are fire pixels from the 3.9 µm band, as on True Color. Daytime only.

IR Enhanced

True Color with a translucent colour overlay of the 10.4 µm infrared band (B13) by day; infrared only at night. Runs day and night.

15 Nov 2026 03:30 SGT
14 Nov 2026 19:30 UTC
Download PNG · Open full size

Cloud-top temperature, 10.4 µm (°C); warmer than −20 °C is grey

-20-30-40-50-60-70-80-90

Colours follow the CIMSS/SSEC GOES Clean IR Window enhancement, applied to brightness temperature. By day the overlay is clear at −20 °C and 70 % opaque from −40 °C and colder, so the surface stays visible under cloud. Where the sun is down, warm surfaces are grey (black is about +47 °C, an estimate) and everything colder than −20 °C is opaque colour. The two blend by sun angle (True Color in full up to 60° zenith, grey IR at 90°), so dawn and dusk fade smoothly. The infrared band has 2 km pixels (lightly smoothed here). Cold cloud tops are high, thick, usually convective cloud.

Day Fire

NOAA/CIRA Day Fire RGB on Himawari bands (R B07 3.9 µm thermal, scaled 27–60 °C, G B04 0.86 µm, B B03 0.64 µm), gamma 0.4/1/1. Fires show red.

Download PNG · Open full size
  • Fire / hot spot
  • New burn scar
  • Smoke
  • Low / warm cloud
  • Cold cloud (10.4 µm below ~290 K, added)
  • Healthy vegetation
  • Bare ground / old burn scar / urban
  • Water

Recipe from the VIIRS Day Fire RGB quick guide, with the red minimum raised from 0 to 27 °C so tropical sea stays black. Over forest, smoke and cloud come out the same teal, so cold cloud (10.4 µm below ~290 K) is shown grey; smoke and low, warm cloud keep their colour. The 0.86/0.64 µm channels are divided by cos(sun zenith), capped at 80°, so brightness does not follow the sun (it overshoots when the sun is more than ~60° from vertical; an amber low-sun note on the image marks that). Himawari's 3.9 µm band has 2 km pixels (VIIRS: 375 m), so only larger fires show. Warm, sunlit ground and sun glint on water can also look red, and thick cloud hides fires.

Smoke RGB

ASMC-style smoke and haze composite (R B03 0.64 µm, G B04 0.86 µm, B B13 10.4 µm inverted). Telling low cloud and haze from thicker or higher cloud.

14 Nov 2026 16:00 SGT
14 Nov 2026 08:00 UTC
Download PNG · Open full size
  • Haze, smoke and low (warm) cloud
  • Thick or high (cold) cloud: white / blue-white
  • Land and vegetation
  • Sea (clear)
  • Fire pixel (3.9 µm, red mark)

Land is green (strong near-infrared). Smoke, haze and low, warm cloud are yellow: they show in the visible and near-infrared channels but not in the infrared. Thick or high cloud is cold, so it is also bright in the infrared channel and comes out white or blue-white. The same colour assignment is not unique: warm, thick low cloud and fog also look yellow, so use the texture (smoke is smooth and streaky, cloud is lumpy). The ranges (red 0–45 %, green 0–65 %, blue 305–220 K) were tuned here, as the guide gives none; the 0.64/0.86 µm channels are divided by cos(sun zenith), capped at 80°, like Day Fire (an amber note marks a low sun). Red marks are fire pixels from the 3.9 µm band, as on True Color. Daytime only.
