Cloud-top temperature, 10.4 µm (°C); warmer than −20 °C is grey

-20-30-40-50-60-70-80-90

Colours follow the CIMSS/SSEC GOES Clean IR Window enhancement, applied to brightness temperature. By day the overlay is clear at −20 °C and 70 % opaque from −40 °C and colder, so the surface stays visible under cloud. Where the sun is down, warm surfaces are grey (black is about +47 °C, an estimate) and everything colder than −20 °C is opaque colour. The two blend by sun angle (True Color in full up to 60° zenith, grey IR at 90°), so dawn and dusk fade smoothly. The infrared band has 2 km pixels (lightly smoothed here). Cold cloud tops are high, thick, usually convective cloud.

Day Fire

NOAA/CIRA Day Fire RGB on Himawari bands (R B07 3.9 µm thermal, scaled 27–60 °C, G B04 0.86 µm, B B03 0.64 µm), gamma 0.4/1/1. Fires show red.

  • Cold cloud (10.4 µm below ~290 K, added)

Recipe from the VIIRS Day Fire RGB quick guide, with the red minimum raised from 0 to 27 °C so tropical sea stays black. Over forest, smoke and cloud come out the same teal, so cold cloud (10.4 µm below ~290 K) is shown grey; smoke and low, warm cloud keep their colour. The 0.86/0.64 µm channels are divided by cos(sun zenith), capped at 80°, so brightness does not follow the sun (it overshoots when the sun is more than ~60° from vertical; an amber low-sun note on the image marks that). Himawari's 3.9 µm band has 2 km pixels (VIIRS: 375 m), so only larger fires show. Warm, sunlit ground and sun glint on water can also look red, and thick cloud hides fires.
